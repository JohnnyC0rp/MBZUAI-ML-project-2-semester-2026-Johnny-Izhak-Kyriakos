\section{Feature Engineering}

Most of the score jump came from feature work rather than from replacing one learner with another. The original columns already described income, debt, assets, and credit history, but many of the strongest relationships only became clear after the variables were expressed as lending ratios, delinquency summaries, or context-aware flags.

        \midrule

        Delinquency summaries & weighted late-payment counts, late payments per history year, utilization interactions & They were the most reliable features for separating higher risk tiers and explaining large APR jumps. \\
        Derogatory burden & charge-off, collection, bankruptcy, and public-record aggregates & A compact summary of past credit damage that avoided over-relying on any single raw count. \\

Table~\ref{tab:feature-deltas} tracks the large additions that moved the validation score during the project. The first major step was the jump from the assignment baseline to the ratio-and-missingness feature set. Controlled polynomial terms gave only a small extra gain, which was a useful warning that feature count alone was not the answer. The next substantial jump came from combining different tree families on top of the engineered table. Later gains from target statistics and the neural branch were real, but smaller.

Figure~\ref{fig:feature-signal} shows the target-specific ranking of the strongest engineered variables. The two tasks clearly overlap, but they do not react to exactly the same form of signal. For classification, the most useful variables are raw delinquency counts, utilization, and their interaction terms. For regression, the ordering leans even more strongly toward severity interactions such as \texttt{LateSeverityXUtilization}. In other words, the same borrower history matters for both tasks, but the rate model is slightly more sensitive to how several risk signals combine.

The final feature set was therefore not a maximal one. We kept only feature groups that either improved both tasks directly or increased diversity for the ensemble stage.
